# Objection Responses

For each common objection: what it sounds like, what's really behind it, and how to respond.

---

## Price: "It's more than we expected."

**What's behind it:** Budget wasn't disclosed, they're comparing to free/cheap alternatives, or they need to justify the spend internally.

**Probe first:** "Help me understand — what range were you thinking, and what are you comparing this to?"

**Response paths:**

- **Scope adjustment:** "I can tailor the scope to fit your budget. For example, [condensed format / virtual delivery / fewer customizations] would bring it to around $[X]. Which element is most important to keep?"
- **Value framing:** "The [Reference Client] engagement at $4,500 delivered [outcome context]. For [their audience], this is comparable. Organizations typically see this as [investment in capability / productivity / competitive edge], not just a training cost."
- **ROI framing:** "With [N] participants, that's about $[per-person]. If each person finds one workflow improvement that saves them 30 minutes a week, the workshop pays for itself in [rough math]."
- **Payment flexibility:** "I can split this into [2–3 payments] if that helps with your budget cycle."

---

## Relevance: "Will this actually work for our people?"

**What's behind it:** They've had bad training experiences, their audience is non-technical, or they're worried about engagement.

**Response paths:**

- **[Reference Client] proof:** "I delivered this for [Reference Client], working with entrepreneurs and small-business owners — many with no technical background. The feedback was [relevant testimonial theme]."
- **Testimonial:** "One participant said: '[quote from catalog that matches their audience concern].' That's the experience I aim for."
- **Tailoring:** "I customize examples and exercises for your specific audience. For [their industry/role], I'd use [concrete example of relevant prompt or workflow]."
- **Format reassurance:** "The workshop is structured so beginners feel scaffolded while experienced participants still find depth. It's not a lecture — it's hands-on from the first 20 minutes."

---

## Timing: "We're not ready yet." / "Let's revisit next quarter."

**What's behind it:** Genuine timing issue, competing priorities, or polite brush-off.

**Probe first:** "What would need to be true for the timing to be right?"

**Response paths:**

- **Genuine timing issue:** "Totally fair. Let's pencil [proposed timeframe]. What's the planning lead time for programs like this? I'll reach out [X weeks] before to confirm."
- **Competing priorities:** "Understood. When you do revisit this, what would make it a priority? I want to make sure I'm addressing the real need."
- **Create urgency (if legitimate):** "One thing to consider — I'm booking [X weeks/months] out, and [specific timeframe] tends to fill. If you want to lock a date now with a later start, I'm happy to do that."
- **Plant a seed:** "No rush. In the meantime, I'd love to send you [relevant article / resource / case study] so you have a better sense of what's possible when the time comes."

---

## Customization: "Can you tailor it more to our [industry / team / use case]?"

**What's behind it:** Legitimate need for relevance, or scope creep disguised as enthusiasm.

**Response paths:**

- **Within scope:** "Absolutely — that's what the prep and customization phase is for. I'll build [industry/team]-specific examples into the exercises and materials. That's included in the proposal."
- **Past scope threshold:** "I can go deeper on [specific customization], but that level of tailoring adds [X hours] of prep. I'd scope that as a custom addition at $[Y], or we could start with the standard workshop and add a follow-up session focused on [their specific need]."
- **Over-customization flag:** If they're asking for essentially new curriculum, push back: "Here's my closest proven workshop. Starting from that foundation and adapting for your team is faster and more reliable. Building entirely new curriculum for [specific topic] would be a separate engagement at [$X]."

---

## Credibility: "Have you done this before for someone like us?"

**What's behind it:** They want proof that this works for their specific context — industry, audience type, organization size.

**Response paths:**

- **Direct match:** If [Your Name] has relevant experience: "[Client/Org] was similar — [audience, industry context]. Here's what they got out of it: [outcome or testimonial]."
- **Adjacent match:** "While I haven't worked with a [exact industry] organization specifically, I've delivered this for [closest comparable]. The core mechanics work across industries — I customize the examples and exercises for your context."
- **Offer reference call:** "I can ask a past client if they'd be open to a brief reference call. Would that help?"
- **Pilot / smaller start:** "If you'd prefer to test this first, we could do a condensed [2-hour / half-day] version at $[X]. If it lands well, we scope the full engagement."

---

## Internal Buy-In: "I need to get approval from [someone else]."

**What's behind it:** They're not the final decision-maker, or they need cover to sell this internally.

**Response paths:**

- **Arm them:** "What would help you make the case internally? I can put together a one-page summary, a sample agenda, or join a 15-minute call with [decision-maker] if that's useful."
- **Specific materials:** "I'll send you a brief deck with: what participants actually do, expected outcomes, proof points from comparable engagements, and the pricing. Use whatever's helpful."
- **Decision-maker meeting:** "Happy to do a quick call with [decision-maker] — sometimes it helps for them to hear directly how this works and what the ROI looks like."
- **Risk reversal:** "If it helps with approval, we can structure this with a smaller deposit upfront, or a satisfaction clause — if after the first hour it's not working, we stop and you only pay for time delivered."

---

## Competition: "We're also talking to [competitor / internal team / free resource]."

**What's behind it:** They're comparison shopping, or they think they can get this cheaper.

**Probe first:** "What's attractive about that option, and what would make our approach the better fit?"

**Response paths:**

- **Internal vs. external:** "Internal teams know your context but may not have the hands-on teaching reps I bring. The value of an external facilitator is fresh perspective, dedicated prep, and the ability to push people past their comfort zone without office dynamics."
- **Free resource (YouTube, online course):** "Those are great for self-directed learning. A workshop is different — it's live, interactive, tailored to your team's actual workflows, and creates shared language and momentum across the group. You're paying for the outcome, not the information."
- **Competitor:** "I don't know their approach, but here's what's different about mine: [specific differentiator — hands-on build component, product thinking framework, community access, post-workshop support]. What matters most to you?"

---

## Procurement: "We need a formal vendor registration / PO / net-60 terms."

**What's behind it:** Standard corporate process. Not an objection — it's a procedural delay.

**Response paths:**

- **Accommodate:** "Happy to go through your vendor process. Can you send me the registration link or form, and let me know the typical timeline?"
- **Parallel track:** "Let's start that process now so it doesn't delay the engagement. In parallel, can we confirm the dates and scope so I can begin prep once registration clears?"
- **Deposit protection:** "Standard payment is 50% deposit to confirm dates, balance net-15 after delivery. If your PO process requires net-60, I can accommodate for established organizations, but the deposit still applies."

---

## "Can you do it for less? We're a nonprofit / we have a limited budget."

**What's behind it:** Genuine budget constraint vs. negotiation tactic.

**Probe first:** "What's the budget you're working with? I want to see if there's a way to make this work."

**Response paths:**

- **Scope down, not price down:** "I can adjust the scope: [condensed format / fewer sessions / virtual delivery / less customization]. That would bring it to around $[X], which may fit better."
- **Trade for value:** "If budget is tight, here's what I can do at $[lower price] if you're willing to [provide testimonial / make an introduction / agree to a case study / commit to two sessions]."
- **Strategic discount (use sparingly):** "I'm making an exception here because [specific strategic reason — ecosystem alignment, mission fit, pipeline-building]. This is $[X] instead of the standard $[Y]."
- **Walk away if needed:** "I understand. My floor for this engagement is $[X], and I can't go below that without sacrificing quality. If your budget opens up in the future, I'd love to revisit."

---

## "Let me think about it."

**What's behind it:** Genuine deliberation, or discomfort saying no directly.

**Response path:**

"I appreciate that. What specifically do you want to think through? If it's [price / timing / fit / internal alignment], I might be able to help. And what's a realistic timeline for a decision so I'm not guessing?"

If they can't name what they're thinking about, the deal is likely not real. Don't chase.
